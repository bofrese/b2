# LinkedIn Comment Reply Principles

Reference guide for drafting substantive, on-voice replies to LinkedIn comments.

---

## What Makes a Good Reply

A reply earns its place by doing one of these things:

1. **Extends the idea** - takes the commenter's observation one level deeper
2. **Reframes it** - names the underlying mechanism they were describing without knowing it
3. **Challenges it** - respectfully disagrees and explains why
4. **Adds specificity** - takes a general claim and grounds it in something concrete
5. **Personalises it** - connects the commenter's story to a pattern the author recognises

A reply that only validates ("Great point!") wastes the distribution opportunity and signals nothing about the author's thinking.

---

## Structural Anti-Patterns

- **The opener trap** - "Great insight!", "Love this!", "Totally agree!" - signals nothing, adds nothing
- **The pitch** - using the reply to mention services, methodology, or redirect to a website
- **The lecture** - moralising at the commenter instead of engaging with their specific point
- **The aphorism stack** - bullet lists of polished insights. Reads as AI or content-marketing, not conversation
- **The engagement bait close** - "What do you think?" or "Have you seen this too?" at the end. Transparent and unnecessary.

---

## Tone Calibration

Tone should match:
- **Relationship:** If the author knows the commenter, write as if talking to them directly. Warmer, more casual.
- **Comment type:** A personal story → empathetic + structural insight. A challenge → engage it honestly. A question → answer it with depth.
- **Sensitivity:** If the comment describes someone making a mistake, frame it as a hard problem, not a character flaw. Structural causes are more useful than personal blame.

---

## Using Related Post Material

Related posts linked in a `## See also` section are the intellectual scaffolding behind the original post. When a related post contains a sharp line that applies directly to the comment, use it - verbatim if it's clean. This deepens the reply without requiring new invention.

The goal: elevate the commenter's observation by connecting it to something structural - the layer beneath what they described.

---

## Length and Format

- 2–4 short paragraphs
- One idea per paragraph
- No bullet lists
- No headers
- Dry, direct closing line - not uplifting, not a question
